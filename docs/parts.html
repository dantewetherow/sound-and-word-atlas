Parts

A dictionary files words by their first letter, which scatters a part across the alphabet. Here each part gathers its words, wherever it sits in them, so the meaning they share shows.

364 words are taken apart so far, 171 of them as a word sum only, with no marks yet. A word that’s marked opens in Words.

        -ionthe act or result of25 words

        It’s -ion, not -tion

        The t in action belongs to act. The suffix is -ion: act + ion, collect + ion, product + ion. Taught as -tion, the base loses its last letter and its family goes out of sight: ac isn’t the base, and act is: it is in actor, active and react.

        This is also why the letter before it varies. It is the base’s own letter: t in act + ion, s in discuss + ion and tens(e) + ion. A speller who finds the base knows which letter to write, and one who memorizes -tion and -sion as endings has to guess.

        What the i does is join the letter before it. T and i together spell the sh sound, so the notation gives them one green bond bar across the seam, the t still black and the i still red. In opinion and union the i spells the y sound instead, and nothing before it changes.

        Where a verb ends in -ate, the longer ending is two parts with the e dropped: educat(e) + ion, notat(e) + ion. Where there is no such verb, as in inform + ation, English uses -ation whole.

        And -ion moves the stress to the syllable just before it: EDucate, eduCAtion. More on that is with produce and production.
