Legomorphic Notation

A set of marks and colours drawn on ordinary spelling, so that what a reader usually works out alone is there for everyone to see.

From note to notation

notenot(e) + ate → notatenotat(e) + ion → notation

The silent e sends a vault back, so the o says its name.The e drops at the seam, and the a of ate takes over its job.The e drops again. The i of ion takes over the vault, and says sh with the t, right across the seam.

The route at a glance

The route has thirty-two steps in eight bands. Each mark enters with the first word that needs it, and the verb kernel runs through every band. Open a step to see its words marked, the marks it brings in, and the pages in Carney's survey that back it.

Each word is shown with its letters in colour: red for vowels, green for sh, th and ch, black for the rest.

About this version

What's here

The route: 32 steps in 8 bands, 237 different words, 220 of them marked. Printable cards for the ELP high-frequency words and all of Dolch’s sight words are on the Word cards page, and Word lists shows where the ELP words come from. The first session of the staff series is under Presentations. The legend in full, the word families and the teaching routines come next.

Where it comes from

The route and its notes come from the Legomorphic Notation manual, and the site is rebuilt from it, so corrections go into the manual first. The marks are drawn with the same tools as the session decks. About explains the notation and its name, and Credits names the writers it draws on.
