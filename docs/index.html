Sound and Word Atlas

Sounds first: a circle for each sound.Red for the vowel sounds.A swoop for each syllable: leg, o, mor, phic.Bars where letters spell a single sound: or and ph.Then the letters.A cup over a short vowel, a bar over a long one.Seams where the parts join: leg + o + morph + ic. The o is a part of its own, meaning “I,” so it spells its name, as in go.

Legomorphic Notation

A set of marks and colours drawn on ordinary spelling, so that what a reader usually works out alone is there for everyone to see.

From note to notation

notenot(e) + ate → notatenotat(e) + ion → notation

The silent e sends a vault back, so the o spells its name.The e drops at the seam, and the a of ate takes over its job.The e drops again. The i of ion takes over the vault, and spells sh with the t, right across the seam.

What’s here

The route

32 steps in 8 bands, with 242 different words, 225 of them marked, in Steps. Each step page has its words, the marks it brings in, and the pages in Carney’s survey that back it.

To print and present

For teachers has printable cards for the ELP high-frequency words and all of Dolch’s sight words, and the first session of the staff series with its handouts. Connections, there too, lines the route up with the ELP and Dolch lists and with UFLI Foundations.

The notation

The marks are with the steps. On the teachers’ shelf, About explains the notation and its name, Definitions has its words, and Credits names the writers it draws on.
