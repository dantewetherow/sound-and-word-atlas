Credits and copyright

Legomorphic notation stands on other people’s work. These are the writers it draws on, and the terms for using what’s here.

Spelling and words

  • Edward Carney, A Survey of English Spelling (Routledge, 1994). The backbone of the route, cited by page on every step.
  • Melvyn Ramsden, whose Real Spelling gave us word sums and word matrices, the basis of the grade 3 word sum cards. His book is Rescuing Spelling (Southgate Publishers).
  • Pete Bowers, who brought word sums and matrices into classrooms as structured word inquiry. His book is Teaching How the Written Word Works. With John R. Kirby and S. Hélène Deacon he wrote “The Effects of Morphological Instruction on Literacy Skills: A Systematic Review of the Literature,” Review of Educational Research 80, no. 2 (2010) (doi), and with Jeffrey Bowers, “Beyond Phonics: The Case for Teaching Children the Logic of the English Spelling System,” Educational Psychologist 52, no. 2 (2017): 124–141 (doi).
  • Sue Scibetta Hegland, Beneath the Surface of Words: What English Spelling Reveals and Why It Matters (Learning About Spelling).
  • E. W. Dolch, “A Basic Sight Vocabulary,” The Elementary School Journal 36, no. 6 (1936): 456–460. The Dolch cards and the Word lists page.
  • Edward Fry, whose first 100 Instant Words hold the three ELP words that aren’t Dolch’s: each, other and people.
  • Central Okanagan Public Schools, Early Literacy Profile (ELP) Assessment & Instructional Guide (2025), pp. 16–17, for the ELP word lists. The cards made from them aren’t an SD23 publication.

Sounds and learning

  • Daniil Elkonin. Sound circles go back to his boxes, where a child pushes a counter into a box for each sound.
  • The studies behind marking by hand are cited on Using the operator cards, in the Session 1 handouts.

Copywrite

Children learn language by copying it: the voices around them first, then letters and words, until the copies become something they can write for themselves. Every language user does the same, and so does anyone who builds on a language, whether in a lesson, a book or a notation like this.

Copyright guards a particular way of saying something. Copywrite is the older practice underneath it: taking what others have said and written, and writing it again in your own hand. This site is copywrite too, written in its own marks from what the people above worked out. The licence is meant to keep that open. Copy these cards, mark them, change them, and make them fit your learners.
