For teachers · Extension

Affix tent cards

Cards that stand up on a desk: prefixes, roots, suffixes, and cards for the joins that change something. Stand a prefix, a root and a suffix in a row and read the word. Then keep the affixes and swap the root. The affixes are few and come back again and again; the roots change.

Print the cards: two to a page. Which rows make words is below them. Cut each page in half, fold each half along the dotted line, and stand it up. Both sides show the part; the side that faces you also says what it means and how it joins.

Some prefixes change their last letter to match the root they join. The card says when.

* A join the process cards don’t cover: ex + spect drops its s (expect), and sub- turns to sup- and sus- (support, suspect). A good one to puzzle over. Some cells are the same root with an older meaning: remedy (re + med + y) is healing again; indict keeps a silent c and is said like invite.

Using them

Start with one root and three or four affixes, and let learners find the real words. Then keep the affixes and swap the root: con + struct + ion, con + duct + ion, con + tract + ion, con + ject + ion? Not every row makes a word, and that last one doesn’t. Asking which ones do, and checking a dictionary, is the inquiry.

When a join changes a letter, a process card goes at the seam. Produce + er needs the (e) card: produc(e) + er. In + lumin + ate needs the prefix card: il + lumin + ate.

The word families are where these come from: *leg-, *deuk-, *sem-, *med- and *leuk-, each with its own spelling list.

Meanings: Etymonline. Word sums and matrices come from Melvyn Ramsden’s Real Spelling, brought into classrooms by Peter Bowers as structured word inquiry.
