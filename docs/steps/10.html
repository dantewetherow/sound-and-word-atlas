The nice-nudge

Step 10

Open this step on the board: the words big, the notes hidden.

A final e

An e, i or y right after

Contrast

Affixes and roots

The final e nudges the c soft, as nice; in duce it sends the vault too. Each is marked as it’s said on its own; inside a word many are said weakly.

New marks

nice-nudge, from a final e and from an e, i or y right after the c or g

The move

Look at the letter right after the c or g. An e, i or y nudges it soft: c says /s/ and g says /j/. Any other letter, or none, and it stays hard. The sentence to remember: The gentle giant went to the gym by cycling to the city centre. For c it nearly always holds. For g it usually does, but some old English words keep a hard g before e or i: get, give, girl, begin. Try soft first, then hard.

Kernel words

None at this step.

Carney (1994)

c spells /s/ before e (rule C.8), p. 303; g spells /j/ before e, i and y (rules G.9 and G.12), pp. 326–327

Research

Carney’s rules: c spells /s/ before e (C.8), p. 303, and before i (city is one of his examples), p. 304; g spells /j/ before e (G.9), p. 326, and before i and y (G.12), p. 327, except before a further r and a consonant (girl), with native words such as get and give keeping a hard g.
