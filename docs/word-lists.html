Word lists

Where the ELP words come from

The ELP guide names no source for its high-frequency lists, but 145 of its 148 words are E. W. Dolch’s, from the sight vocabulary he published in 1936. The other three (each, other, people) are on Fry’s first 100 Instant Words. The ELP keeps 144 of Dolch’s 220 words, sorts them by spelling pattern, and leaves out 76, most of them short-vowel words that phonics lessons teach.

Dolch’s 95 nouns are left out of the charts and the table. He kept them off his sight vocabulary, and water is the only one on the ELP lists.

What the ELP leaves out

53 of the 76 are one-syllable short-vowel words, such as big, can, run, stop, best and six. Apart from and, is and it in kindergarten, the ELP’s only short-vowel words are on its Digraphs list, so it seems to leave the rest to phonics lessons.

Pattern before level

A pattern list takes the words that share its pattern, whatever their Dolch level: 74 of the 144 shared words are on one. The silent e list pulls five, gave, made, these, those, use and write into grade 1 from Dolch’s second grade, while the diphthong list holds 10 early words such as how, round and down back to grade 2.

Levels aren’t grades

Pre-primer and primer are named for reading books, and the Utah Reading Clinic puts Dolch’s first three levels all in grade 1. Read that way, the ELP has 61 of the shared words earlier than Dolch, 66 in the same grade and 17 later.

The cards

All five of Dolch’s levels are on the Word cards page, coded on the back. Each card’s tag names the ELP list that teaches the word, if one does.
