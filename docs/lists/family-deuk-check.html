Check: The *deuk- family: produce, product, production, reduce

Is the base spelled duce for the verb and duct before an ending, with the prefix kept whole?

Print the check: a sheet for the learner, then the words to say and the key.

Spelling check Name Date

Write each word you hear.

These are made-up words. Add the ending.

  1. ab + duce →

To say, and the key

Say the word, then the sentence, then the word again. For the made-up words, say: “These are made-up words. The first part is printed for you. Add the ending and write the whole word.” Don’t teach these words between checks.

Score each word twice: the whole word right, and the feature right. A learner can have the feature right and the word wrong, or the other way round.

Reading the result

Give the check before the lesson, after it, and again a few weeks later. The lesson worked if the transfer and invented words rise and the control words don’t rise as much. If everything rises together, something other than the lesson did it. If the limit words fall, the convention was over-learned: it is being used where it doesn’t belong.

Use words the learners already say. If most can spell a word beforehand, it can’t show anything; swap it for another of the same kind.

With two classes, compare the gain on transfer words against the gain on control words in each. That is suggestive, not proof: the classes differ in more than the lesson. A stronger design needs only one class: teach three lessons in turn, give all three checks each time, and see whether each set of transfer words rises only after its own lesson.

Keep learners’ results in the school’s own records. Nothing here collects them.
