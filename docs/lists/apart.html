Spelled apart

Words that sound the same and are spelled differently. In a sentence, the grammar tells them apart; on the page, the spelling does. Marked side by side, the difference shows: the h on the bar of hour, the second e of bee.

Say both sentences, then write the word that fits each. Ask which group the pair is in: a short word and a long one, a letter kept from the family, history, or a squeezed-out vowel. A spelling list of pairs tests something the other kinds don’t: telling two spellings apart, not learning one.

1. Length marks the job

A small grammar word can have one or two letters; a word that names a thing or an action takes three or more. The length tells you the word’s job before the sentence does (Carney 1994: 131–133).

Bee doubles its e to make three letters. Step 3

Buy and bye both add a letter; the u of buy is silent with the b. Step 3

Inn doubles its n. Step 2

2. A letter kept from the family

The spelling keeps a letter that another form of the word, or a word in its family, says or uses.

Know keeps the k it shares with knowledge and known. Step 25

Knew is the past of know, so it keeps the k. Ablaut: know, knew, known. Step 25

Blew is the past of blow, as grew is of grow and knew of know. Step 21

Two keeps the w that twin, twelve and twenty say. Step 21

Write keeps the w that wrote and written keep. It was once said. Step 25

3. History keeps them apart

These were said differently once. The sounds came together and the spellings didn’t, so now the spelling tells a reader which word it is. That helps, but nobody planned it.

ea and ee once spelled two different vowels. Step 20

The same two vowels. Step 20

The k of knight was once said. Step 25

Son has the o that scribes wrote for u beside m, n and v. Step 19

The h of hour came from Latin hora and was already silent in the French that brought it. It sits on the vowel’s bar. Step 25

4. The apostrophe catastrophe

One of the pair is two words with a vowel squeezed out. Say it as the two words: if that makes sense, the apostrophe goes in, where the vowel was. See Squeezed into oblivion.

It’s is it is. Its has nothing squeezed out, like his. Step 27

You’re is you are. Step 27

They’re is they are. There has here inside it, as where does: the place words. Step 27

Who’s is who is. Step 27
