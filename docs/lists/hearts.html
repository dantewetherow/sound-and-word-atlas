Words with a heart

A heart word has a part a learner can’t decode yet, or can’t decode by the usual sounds at all. A heart over that part says learn this by heart. The notation can usually say why instead. Most heart words are regular once their step comes, or have a reason a learner can use; only a few have to be learned by heart.

Map every heart word sound by sound, as with any word: a circle for each sound, then find the part that doesn’t say what the letters usually say. Then ask which group it’s in. Every word here can be looked up in Words.

1. The heart goes away on the route

These are regular once their step comes. A heart on them would teach that something regular is odd.

ould, one spelling for three words (step 7)

Learn one and spell three.

A long vowel before two consonants (step 18)

Like most, old and find.

al for the vowel of saw (step 23)

The l is silent and joins the vowel.

An r-controlled unit (steps 12 and 26)

The unit spells the sound; several units spell more than one.

Parts that keep their spelling (seams)

Each part is a word the learner knows.

Nothing irregular at all

Very is closed, short e, then y. Been is a team, said with the vowel of bin in many places.

2. The heart part has a reason

A word it comes from, a pattern with a history, or a letter doing a job for another letter.

From a word the learner knows

Say, do, go, true, build: the spelling keeps the word, and the sound moves.

o for the vowel of up, beside m, n, v and th (step 19)

Old handwriting ran u into m, n and v, so scribes wrote o (Carney 1994: 69). One pattern, not thirteen hearts.

o for the vowel of moon, and a after w (step 19)

Two keeps the w that twin, twelve and twenty say.

gu keeps g hard

Without the u, gide would read as jide: g before e, i or y is often soft (step 10). The bond bar shows the u working with the g. Before a, o or u the g is hard anyway, so guard doesn’t need its u; it came with the word from French.

A team saying another of its sounds (step 22)

The bar stays: the spelling is the unit, not the sound.

3. By heart

The heart is honest here. A story helps the word stick, but it won’t help a learner read the next one.

A letter saying another letter’s sound

Of and off were once one word; the f stayed. Busy is spelled with u and said with short i; in build, as in buy, the u is silent beside the b.

One and once

Alone and only keep the old sound of one: all one, one-ly.

A silent letter with nothing to join

The h of hour and honest was silent in the French they came from. Answer is and + swear in Old English. Wednesday is Woden’s day.

ough and augh

Learned by family: tough, rough, enough rhyme.

Others

Again, about and always start with a weak a; past it, they are regular. People keeps an o that popular says.

Most of these words are on the Early Literacy Profile (Central Okanagan) and Dolch lists. Programs such as UFLI Foundations teach them as heart words, with a heart over the part to learn by heart; the notation shows the reason where there is one. Carney, A Survey of English Spelling (1994): o for u beside m, n and v, p. 69; the vowel of book in could, p. 146.
