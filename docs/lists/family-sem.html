Legomorphic spelling · One root, many words

The *sem- family: similar, similarity, assemble, resemble

*sem- means one, as one, together. Its Latin spellings are simil (like), semble (together) and sim (simple); its Greek one is homo (the same).

This list goes with The *sem- family.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

simil + ar → similarYour hats are similar.
simil + ar + ity → similarityThere is a similarity between them.
as + semble → assembleAssemble the robot.
re + semble → resembleYou resemble your dad.
re + sembl(e) + ance → resemblanceI see the resemblance.
sim + ple → simpleThe game is simple.
homo + phone → homophoneTwo and too are homophones.
as + simil + ate → assimilateSome prefixes assimilate to the letter after them.

The contrast

as- before s doubles the s: as + semble, as + simil + ate.

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 11 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

Assimilate names the very thing its spelling shows: ad- became as- to match the s after it.

The check ends with homonym: Greek homo, the same, with nym, a name. English’s own words from the root, same and some, have no parts to build.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
