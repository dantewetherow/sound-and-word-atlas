Spelling lists · A step’s pattern

Step 18: Long before two consonants

Before ld, nd and a few other pairs, i and o often say their names though nothing sends a vault: find, old.

This list goes with Step 18: Long before two consonants.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

My dog is old.
It is cold outside.
Both of us came.
Most cats like milk.
I can’t find my shoe.
Be kind.
The child is happy.
A wild horse ran.

The contrast

The same shape, a different sound: i is long before nd, short before nt.

Build it

  1. f + i + nd →
  2. c + o + ld →
  3. ch + i + ld →
  4. m + o + st →
  5. w + i + ld →
  6. b + o + th →

Use it

  1. Be (kind or kid) to others.
  2. Wear a coat, it’s (cold or cot) .
  3. Can you (find or fin) it?
  4. (Most or Must) of the cake is gone.

Check: not on the list

  1. be + hind →behind
  2. g + o + ld →gold
  3. p + o + st →post
  4. w + i + nd →wind

For the teacher

Carney lists these as long before nd, ld and lt (p. 129; rules I.11 and I.12, pp. 332–333). It is a tendency, not a rule: cost and mint are short.

The check ends with wind, which is long in wind the clock and short in the wind blows.

Have the learner say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.

Source: Edward Carney, A Survey of English Spelling (1994): Long vowels before nd, ld and lt (find, cold, bolt), p. 129; old, olt and oll words, p. 175; find, kind, child and wild (rules I.11 and I.12), pp. 332–333.
