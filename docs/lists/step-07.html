Legomorphic spelling · A step’s pattern

Step 7: One analog, three words

Learn one, spell three. would, could and should share ould: the ou says the vowel of book and the l is silent.

This list goes with Step 7: One analog, three words.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

Would you like a snack?
I could see the moon.
You should rest.

The contrast

The analog is the spelling, not the sound: wood sounds like would and is spelled another way.

Build it

  1. w + ould →
  2. c + ould →
  3. sh + ould →
  4. would + n’t →
  5. could + n’t →
  6. should + n’t →

Use it

  1. (Would or Wood) you help me?
  2. I (could or cold) not find it.
  3. We (should or shoulder) go now.
  4. The fire needs more (wood or would) .

Check: not on the list

  1. would + n’t →wouldn’t
  2. could + n’t →couldn’t
  3. should + n’t →shouldn’t
  4. sh + oulder →shoulder

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 12 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

Three words, one spelling to learn. The l in ould is empty: it has no sound (Carney, p. 249), and nothing in the family brings it back, so it stays in the unit.

The check ends with shoulder, where ould says its letters’ own sounds: the analog stops there.

Have the learner say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
