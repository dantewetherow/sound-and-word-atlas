Legomorphic spelling · A step’s pattern

Step 13: A suffix joins at the seam

A suffix joins at the seam and the base keeps its spelling: go + ing, play + ed. -ed and -s keep one spelling whatever they sound like.

This list goes with Step 13: A suffix joins at the seam.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

get + s → getsHe gets up early.
see + s → seesShe sees the bus.
go + ing → goingWe are going home.
do + ing → doingWhat are you doing?
be + ing → beingThank you for being kind.
play + ed → playedWe played tag.
jumpedjump + ed → jumpedThe frog jumped.
want + ed → wantedI wanted a turn.

The contrast

-ed says three things and is spelled one way.

Use it

  1. Yesterday we (play) outside.
  2. She (see) the moon every night.
  3. We are (go) to school.
  4. He (get) the mail every day.

Did it generalize?

For the teacher

Learners who spell jumped as jumpt are spelling the sound. The seam shows the suffix: one spelling, three sounds (Carney, pp. 18–19).

The check ends with hopping: a short word doubles its last letter before the suffix. That is step 14.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
