Step 19: A vowel that borrows a sound

Some vowels borrow another vowel’s sound: the o of come says the u of up, the a of was says the o of on. Spell them by the word, not the sound.

This list goes with Step 19: A vowel that borrows a sound.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

Come here.
Have some juice.
I am done.
A card from Gran.
It was fun.
What is that?
Do you want it?
Who is there?

The contrast

The same letters, a borrowed sound.

Build it

  1. s + o + me →
  2. d + o + ne →
  3. fr + o + m →
  4. w + a + s →
  5. wh + a + t →
  6. t + o →

Use it

  1. Can you (come or comb) over?
  2. I want (some or sum) milk.
  3. This is (from or form) my mom.
  4. (Who or How) ate my sandwich?

Check: not on the list

  1. l + o + ve →love
  2. m + o + n + ey →money
  3. n + o + ne →none
  4. h + o + me →home

For the teacher

Next to m and n, old handwriting ran u into its neighbours, so scribes wrote o instead: come, some, money (Carney, p. 69).

The check ends with home, which keeps o’s own sound: the borrowing isn’t a rule to apply to every o.

Have the learner say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.

Source: Edward Carney, A Survey of English Spelling (1994): o for u beside m and n, where pen strokes ran together (come), p. 69; a after w (want, wash, what, was), p. 150; do, to and who, p. 198.
