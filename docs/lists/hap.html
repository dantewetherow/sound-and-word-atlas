Spelling list · One base, many joins

hap: happy, happen, mishap

Hap is an old word for luck. Before a vowel it is written happ, so the a stays short. Before a consonant, or at the end of a word, it is hap.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

hap + p + y → happyI am happy.
hap + p + (y→i) + er → happierShe is happier now.
hap + p + (y→i) + ly → happilyThey played happily.
un + hap + p + y → unhappyHe was unhappy.
hap + p + en → happenWhat will happen?
mis + hap → mishapIt was a small mishap.
hap + less → haplessThe hapless cat fell in.

The contrast

The p doubles only before a vowel.

Use it

  1. She smiled (happy + ly) .
  2. He is (happy + er) than before.
  3. Did anything (hap + en) ?
  4. It was only a (mis + hap) .

Check: not on the list

  1. hap + y + ness →happiness
  2. hap + en + ed →happened
  3. per + hap + s →perhaps
  4. un + hap + y + ly →unhappily

For the teacher

Every word here has the same base, with the same meaning, so the family shows the seam that happy alone hides: hap + y, having luck. The double p isn’t a second sound. It keeps the a short before the vowel that follows.

In mishap the s and the h meet across a seam, so they don’t spell sh.

The check asks for the y to turn to i (happiness, unhappily) and for perhaps, where hap is in the middle with one p.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
