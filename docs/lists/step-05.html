Step 5: Two letters, one sound

Some sounds are spelled with two letters: th, sh, ch, wh. The two letters make one sound, under one circle.

This list goes with Step 5: Two letters, one sound.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

The sun is hot.
She can read.
This is my book.
That dog is big.
Come with me.
Thank you so much.
It was such fun.
Which one is yours?

The contrast

She is open and long. Add a d and it closes: shed.

Build it

  1. th + e →
  2. sh + e →
  3. th + i + s →
  4. w + i + th →
  5. m + u + ch →
  6. wh + i + ch →

Use it

  1. Can I come (with or which) you?
  2. Thank you very (much or such) !
  3. (She or The) is my sister.
  4. (Which or With) way is home?

Check: not on the list

  1. sh + i + p →ship
  2. ch + i + n →chin
  3. th + i + n →thin
  4. mis + hap →mishap

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 13 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

The h in th, sh and ch is a helper: it changes the letter before it instead of saying its own sound (Carney, p. 39).

The th of the, this and that buzzes; the th of thin doesn’t. Same spelling, two sounds.

The check ends with mishap, where s and h meet across a seam and don’t spell sh.

Have the learner say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
