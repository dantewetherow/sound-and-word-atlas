Spelling lists · A step’s pattern

Step 10: The nice-nudge

An e, i or y right after a c or g nudges it soft: c says /s/, g says /j/. The gentle giant went to the gym by cycling to the city centre.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

That is nice.
Wash your face.
What is your age?
Turn the page.
We live in a city.
We play in the gym.
The giant was tall.
It costs one cent.

The contrast

Hard before a, o and u; soft before e, i and y.

Build it

  1. pl + a + ce →
  2. r + a + ge →
  3. c + e + ll →
  4. g + e + m →
  5. c + i + t + y →
  6. h + u + ge →

Use it

  1. A (giant or gate) lives in the castle.
  2. Wash your (face or fact) .
  3. We live in a big (city or kitty) .
  4. Turn the (page or pack) .

Check: not on the list

  1. r + i + ce →rice
  2. c + i + r + c + le →circle
  3. g + e + m →gem
  4. g + e + t →get

For the teacher

For c it nearly always holds (Carney’s rule C.8, p. 303). For g it usually does, but some old English words keep a hard g before e or i: get, give, girl, begin. Try soft first, then hard.

The check ends with get, where the nudge doesn’t take.

Have the learner say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.

Source: Edward Carney, A Survey of English Spelling (1994): c spells /s/ before e (rule C.8), p. 303; g spells /j/ before e, i and y (rules G.9 and G.12), pp. 326–327.
