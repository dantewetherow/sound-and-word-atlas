Legomorphic spelling · A step’s pattern

Step 30: un- and re-

un- and re- join at the front, and neither the prefix nor the base changes: un + do, re + fill.

This list goes with Step 30: un- and re-.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

un + do → undoUndo the knot.
un + tie → untieUntie your shoes.
un + hap + p + y → unhappyHe felt unhappy.
un + lock → unlockUnlock the door.
re + do → redoRedo the page.
re + play → replayReplay the song.
re + fill → refillRefill my cup.
re + read → rereadReread the story.

The contrast

The prefix joins before the base; the base keeps its spelling.

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 13 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

Nothing drops or doubles at the front of a word in these: the prefix and the base each keep their letters, which is why unnatural has two n’s.

The check ends with re-enter, where the hyphen keeps two e’s apart (Carney, p. 48).

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
