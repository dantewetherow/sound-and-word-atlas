Spelling lists · One join, many bases

the e drops: making, hoping, icy

A suffix that starts with a vowel takes the place of a silent e. Out with the old, in with the new: the new vowel does the e’s job.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

mak(e) + ing → makingI am making lunch.
tak(e) + ing → takingShe is taking a turn.
giv(e) + ing → givingHe is giving it back.
hav(e) + ing → havingWe are having fun.
rac(e) + ing → racingThey are racing home.
hop(e) + ing → hopingI am hoping for snow.
ic(e) + y → icyThe road is icy.

The contrast

With no e to drop, a short word doubles its last letter instead.

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 16 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

The e of make sends the vault that keeps the a long. In making the i stands where the e stood and sends it. In racing and icy the new vowel also keeps the c soft.

Giving and having drop an e that had a different job: keeping v off the end of the word. Once a suffix follows, the v isn’t at the end.

The check ends with two words where the e stays, hopeful and likely: the suffix starts with a consonant, so nothing new arrives to do the e’s job.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
