The *leuk- family: illustrate, illustration, illuminate, lucid

*leuk- means light. Latin made it lustr (illustrate), lumin (illuminate), luc (lucid) and lun (lunar); English kept light.

This list goes with The *leuk- family.

illuminateil + lumin + ate → illuminateLamps illuminate the street.
lucidluc + id → lucidGive a lucid answer.
translucenttrans + luc + ent → translucentThe glass is translucent.
lunarlun + ar → lunarWe watched a lunar eclipse.

The contrast

Two l’s in both: il + lustr, il + lumin.

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 11 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

In illustrate the prefix is in-, in, into, written il- before l; in illegal it means not. Two prefixes, spelled alike.

The check ends with lightening, making lighter. Lightning, the flash, is the same family with no e. The light that means not heavy sounds the same and comes from another root.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
