Legomorphic spelling

Spelling lists that follow the route: one for every step and every word family. Each has six to eight words with sentences, a contrast, words to build, sentences to finish, and a check of words that aren’t on the list. Print one and the teacher’s notes are left off. Each list also has a generalization check, to give before the lesson, after it and a few weeks later: its list’s page leads to it.

Patterns

Every list is one of four kinds. The first lists were made as models, one of each.

One analog, several wordsOne word’s spelling carries to others that rhyme with it, letter for letter. The model: say, pay, lay.One base, many joinsOne base and the prefixes and suffixes that join it. The model: hap: happy, happen, mishap.One join, many basesOne way that a suffix joins, tried on many bases. The model: the e drops: making, hoping, icy.A closed setA small set of words that work together and change together. The model: this, that, these, those, then, there.

The steps and families mix the kinds: a step about a suffix is one join, many bases; a family is one base, many joins.
