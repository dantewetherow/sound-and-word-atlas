Legomorphic spelling · One root, many words

The *(s)teg- family: protect, protection, protective, protector

tect means covered. Protect is to cover in front; detect, to take the cover off. Every ending and prefix joins tect without changing it; what changes is the sound around the t.

This list goes with The *(s)teg- family.

Word study: Protect, part by part, a presentation for learners.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

pro + tect → protectA fence can protect the garden.
pro + tect + ion → protectionThe roof gives protection from the rain.
pro + tect + ive → protectiveThe lab has protective glasses.
pro + tect + or → protectorA screen protector keeps the glass safe.
pro + tect + ed → protectedThe seeds are protected from the frost.
un + pro + tect + ed → unprotectedThe plants were left unprotected.
over + pro + tect + ive → overprotectiveThe rule felt overprotective.
de + tect → detectCan you detect the smell of smoke?
de + tect + ive → detectiveThe case was solved by a detective.
de + tect + or → detectorThe smoke detector beeped.

The contrast

The t is said in protect. In protection it joins the i of -ion, and ti spells sh.

Check: not on the list

  1. over + pro + tect + ive →overprotective
  2. pro + tect + ive + ness →protectiveness

  4. un + de + tect + ed →undetected

For the teacher

Before a t, Latin’s g became c: tegere, to cover, and tectus, covered. English borrowed the past form, so the part is tect.

After /t/ or /d/, -ed is a syllable of its own: pro-tec-ted. -ive keeps its e before -ly and -ness: protectively, protectiveness.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
