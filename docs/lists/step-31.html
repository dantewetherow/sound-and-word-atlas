Step 31: A Latin family: act and create

A Latin base keeps its spelling through its family: act, act + ion, act + or. It’s -ion, not -tion: the t belongs to act.

This list goes with Step 31: A Latin family: act and create.

The words

actAct it out.
actionact + ion → actionLights, camera, action!
actoract + or → actorShe is an actor.
activeact + ive → activeStay active.
reactre + act → reactHow did he react?
createCreate a picture.
creationcreat(e) + ion → creationWhat a creation!
creatorcreat(e) + or → creatorMeet the creator of the game.

The contrast

The suffix is -ion; the t comes with the base.

Did it generalize?

This list has a check: 12 words to give before the lesson, after it, and a few weeks later. Most of them aren’t on the list, so it shows whether the convention was learned or only the words.

Open the check

For the teacher

Spelling -tion as one suffix hides the base. With -ion, action is act + ion and the t was there all along.

The check ends with actual, where the t of act says /ch/: the base keeps its spelling, not its sound.

Have the learner build each word from its sum, not copy it, and say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.
