Spelling lists · A step’s pattern

open or closed: I, it, in, is, if

A vowel at the end of a syllable is open: it says its name, as I does. A consonant after it closes it, and it says its short sound: it, in, is, if.

Print this list: the teacher’s notes are left off.

The words

I can run.
Say hi to Sam.
It is red.
Come in.
She is here.
Ask if you can go.
Give it to him.
That is his hat.

In is and his the s says /z/. It still closes the syllable.

The contrast

Open, the i says its name. Close it with a consonant and it goes short.

Close it

  1. I, closed with t →
  2. I, closed with n →
  3. I, closed with f →
  4. hi, closed with t →
  5. hi, closed with m →
  6. hi, closed with s →

Use it

  1. Come (in or it) and sit down.
  2. Ask (if or is) you can play.
  3. That is (him or his) bike.
  4. Say (hi or him) to Mom.

Check: not on the list

  1. s + i + t →sit
  2. w + i + n →win
  3. k + i + d →kid
  4. f + i + nd →find

For the teacher

Step 1 has one vowel and one idea. At the end of a syllable the vowel is open and says its name; a consonant after it closes it and it says its short sound. I is open; it, in, is and if are closed.

Hi is the same open i with a consonant in front. Closing it gives hit, him and his: the same move as I to it, so the Close it column is the step’s whole lesson in six words.

I is the one word that is always a capital, wherever it falls in a sentence.

The check uses words that aren’t on the list. Sit, win and kid are closed the same way. Find is where the idea stops: before nd the i stays long though the syllable is closed. That waits for step 18, and a learner who writes it correctly has met it somewhere else.

Have the learner say each sentence before writing it. Six to eight words is the whole list: the convention is what’s being learned.

  • Step 1: open or closed, on the route
  • Step 4: one consonant closes it
  • Step 18: long before two consonants: find

Source: Edward Carney, A Survey of English Spelling (1994): short vowels can’t end an open syllable and long vowels can, p. 134; short i, p. 135; long i, p. 151; s for /z/, p. 238; find and kind, pp. 332–333.
