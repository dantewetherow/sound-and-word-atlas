For teachers

UFLI Foundations

For schools that run UFLI Foundations: where its lessons meet the route, the mark to draw alongside each part of UFLI, and why the two orders differ.

Lesson by lesson, in sixteen bands · Why the orders part · The two orders side by side · Every lesson · Every step

Made to line up with UFLI Foundations' scope and sequence, by lesson number. UFLI didn't make it and hasn't endorsed it. The descriptions are ours. UFLI asks that its materials be linked rather than copied (its copyright statement says so), so its lesson names and word lists stay on its own site: its scope and sequence is in the UFLI Foundations Toolbox.

Lesson by lesson

UFLI’s lessons in sixteen bands. Each band gives what UFLI teaches there, the mark to add, an example or two marked, the steps where the route meets it, and where the two part. Any word from UFLI’s lessons can be looked up in Words.

Lessons 1–12

The first letters, and short a, i and o

The mark. A circle under each sound, the cup over a short vowel, and a swoop under the word.

On the route: steps 1, 2, 4

Where they part. The route opens with I against it: open and closed in the first lesson. UFLI starts a letter at a time, and open syllables wait for lesson 66.

Lessons 13–19

More letters, and short u and e

The mark. The same marks: circles, cups, swoops.

On the route: steps 2, 3, 4

Where they part. Nothing parts here. Both programs are building the closed syllable.

Lessons 20–21

The -s ending, said /s/ and /z/

The mark. The seam: a dotted line before the s. One spelling, two sounds.

On the route: steps 1, 2, 13

Where they part. UFLI is ahead here: -s comes at lesson 20, and on the route at step 13. The seam is the one mark to bring in early.

Lessons 22–41

The rest of the consonants, blends, x and qu

The mark. Two circles under a blend and no bar; the bond bar ties q to u, with a circle under each.

On the route: steps 3, 4, 6

Where they part. The notation draws x with one circle (ruled 8 November 2026), where UFLI counts two sounds. Say both: one letter, two sounds.

Lessons 42–44

ff, ll, ss and zz at the end; all, oll, ull; ck

The mark. A bond bar under the doubled letters. They are the doubled spellings that become a block box when an ending comes.

On the route: steps 6, 14, 23

Where they part. The route ties these to the box (step 14): pick and picking are one idea. UFLI teaches them as spellings at the end.

Lessons 45–53

sh, th, ch, wh, ph, ng and nk

The mark. A green bar and circle for sh, th and ch; a black bar for wh, ph and ng.

On the route: steps 5, 13, 28, 32

Where they part. The route brings these in at step 5, well before UFLI does. One mark serves both sounds of th.

Lessons 54–62

Silent e: a_e, i_e, o_e, e_e, u_e; c and g before e; have and give

The mark. The vowel vault from the e to the vowel it makes long; the nice-nudge to a soft c or g; the cone without a vault in have.

On the route: steps 8, 9, 10

Where they part. UFLI teaches the silent e one vowel at a time. The route teaches one vault for all of them (step 9), and have and give just before it (step 8), so the exception and the rule sit side by side.

Lessons 63–65

-es, -ed and -ing

The mark. The seam before the ending; -ed is one spelling for three sounds.

On the route: step 13

Where they part. UFLI adds the endings here and leaves doubling and the dropped e to lessons 107 to 109. The route brings all three together at steps 13 to 15, so hoping and hopping are learned as one system.

Lessons 66–68

Open and closed syllables, compounds

The mark. The long mark on an open syllable, the cup on a closed one, a swoop for each.

On the route: steps 1, 3, 16

Where they part. The biggest gap. UFLI reaches open syllables at lesson 66; the route starts with them. See why below.

Lessons 69–76

tch and dge; long vowels before two consonants; y as a vowel; -le

The mark. tch and dge as the doubled ch and j, in a box after a short vowel; the long mark with no vault (find); a red circle under y; one swoop and one circle for le.

On the route: steps 3, 6, 14, 17, 18

Where they part. My and by are regular on the route from step 3. In UFLI, y as a vowel waits for lesson 73, and until then they have to be learned as heart words.

Lessons 77–83

ar, or, er, ir, ur, and or after w

The mark. The r-controlled unit: one red bar under the vowel and its r, one circle.

On the route: steps 12, 26

Where they part. The two programs agree. The unit makes visible that er, ir and ur are one sound with three spellings.

Lessons 84–92

Vowel teams: ai, ay, ee, ea, ey, oa, ow, oe, ie, igh, oo, ew, ue

The mark. A red bond bar under the team: two or more letters, one vowel sound.

On the route: steps 11, 20, 21, 22

Where they part. Close agreement. The route adds where a team sits: ai and oa inside a word, ay and ow at the end.

Lessons 93–97

au and aw; ea for short e; oi, oy, ou, ow

The mark. The same red bar, for a team that says a second sound or a sound that glides.

On the route: steps 22, 23, 24

Where they part. Close agreement. The bar stays the same when a team says its second sound (head): the spelling is the unit, not the sound.

Lesson 98

kn, wr and mb

The mark. The silent letter joins its unit under one bar. A cone only where another form of the word says the letter.

On the route: step 25

Where they part. The notation splits UFLI’s silent letters in two: empty ones bonded (knife), inert ones coned (sign, because of signal).

Lessons 99–118

Prefixes and suffixes, doubling, the dropped e, y to i; more vowel spellings

The mark. The seam at each join; the block box for doubling; out with the old where the e drops; y turns to i at the seam.

On the route: steps 10, 13, 14, 15, 20, 21, 26, 29, 30

Where they part. UFLI teaches these late and one rule at a time. The route reads common prefixes early and builds the joins at steps 14, 15, 29 and 30.

Lessons 119–128

-sion, -tion, -ture, -er, -or, -ist, -ness, -ment, -able, -ible; uni-, bi-, tri-

The mark. The seam before the suffix, with the base whole: act + ion, pict + ure. The green bar of ti runs across the seam for the sound.

On the route: steps 29, 31

Where they part. UFLI teaches -tion and -ture as units for the sound. Split at the meaning, the t belongs to the base, which is why it comes back in act, actor and active. The notation shows both at once.

Why the orders part

The route starts with open and closed syllables because the words learners need first are built on that contrast. The prepositions (it, in, on, up), the pronouns (I, me, my, he, she, we) and the pointing words (this, that, these, those) are the grammar of every sentence a learner says and reads. Taught together from the start, they become regular, and the first contrast learners meet is the one the spelling system is built on: I and it, he and hen, no and not.

UFLI comes to open syllables at lesson 66 and to y as a vowel at 73. Until then, words such as he, me and my have to be learned as heart words, by heart, as exceptions. Both programs teach these words early. The difference is that UFLI treats them as exceptions and the route as the rule.

The route also keeps a word’s parts whole. UFLI teaches -tion and -ture as units, which works for saying them: tion reliably says /shun/. The cost is the base. Read as ac + tion, action hides act. Split at the meaning, it is act + ion, and the t belongs to the base, which is why it keeps turning up in actor and active. The notation shows both: the seam before ion, and the green bar of ti across it for the sound.

And it keeps a base’s joins together. UFLI teaches -ing at lesson 65, doubling at 107 and the dropped e at 109. The route puts the vault, the box and the dropped e at steps 9, 14 and 15, so hoping and hopping are one system: the box blocks the vault, and the dropped e lets it through.

Heart words part the same way. UFLI draws a heart over the part to learn by heart. Most heart words are regular once their step comes on the route (could, both, learn), or have a reason a learner can use (said from say; the o of son, month and love); only a few are left to learn by heart. See Words with a heart.

Where UFLI is ahead: the -s ending comes at its lesson 20, before the route’s step 13. The seam is the mark to bring in early.

The two orders side by side

Each dot is a UFLI lesson (across) at the step of the route where it lands (down), with the sixteen bands shaded behind. Dots on the dashed line come in the same order in both programs; dots above it, the route gets to sooner, and dots below it, UFLI does.

Some lessons land on more than one step, so they have more than one dot. Lessons with no dot are reviews, or have no step of their own.
