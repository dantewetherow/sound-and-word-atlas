Roots, and the meanings of parts

A word can be read as it is used now, or traced back through time: synchronic and diachronic, a snapshot and a time-lapse. This door is for the time-lapse. Before is an example: Old English beforan, from be- ‘by’ and foran ‘in front’, and for and fore grow from the same root.

The roots will follow Calvert Watkins’s American Heritage Dictionary of Indo-European Roots (3rd ed., 2011), which is built root first and lists the English words from each root, with links to the Indo-European Lexicon of the Linguistics Research Center at the University of Texas at Austin. Not every word has a known root. Hop and hope go back through Old English to early Germanic, and no further that anyone can show, and their pages will say so.
