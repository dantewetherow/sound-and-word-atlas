Parts · a root family: a word study

*(s)teg-*(s)teg- means ‘to cover’

To cover, often with a roof. Latin made tegere, to cover, and its past form tectus, covered. English borrowed protect, to cover in front, and detect, to take the cover off. It builds on them with almost every common affix: protection, protective, protector, unprotected, overprotective, detective. The part stays tect throughout; the sounds around it change.

Word study: Protect, part by part a presentation for learners, built a piece at a time

These words are built on tect. It means covered.

Say them: pro-tect, pro-tec-tion, pro-tec-tive.

The beat stays on tect every time.

In protection, the t and the i spell /sh/. The t stays in the spelling.

protect ends in /t/, so -ed is a syllable of its own: pro-tec-ted.

A detective takes the cover off: finds out what was covered up.

teg and tect: g becomes c before t

Latin’s verb was tegere, to cover, with a g. Its past form was tectus, covered, with a c. Before the t of the past form, a g became c. The two sounds, g and /k/, are made in the same place; /k/ is g without the voice. Allen and Greenough give the rule: g before t became c (§15.10).

The same change is in other families on this site. Latin legere, to gather, has the past form lectus: legend beside collect. Latin regere, to rule or guide, has rectus: regal beside direct. English borrowed the past forms, so the words built on them spell tect, lect and rect.

One part, and the sounds around it

The spelling of tect never changes in this family. Three sounds next to it do.

The t before -ion. In protect the t is said. In protection it joins the i of -ion, and ti spells sh. The spelling keeps the t, so protection still shows protect.

The -ed after a t. -ed spells /t/ in jumped and /d/ in played. When the word ends in /t/ or /d/, -ed is a syllable of its own: pro-tec-ted, de-tec-ted.

The e of -ive. English words don’t end in v, so -ive has an e that isn’t said and doesn’t make the i long. The e stays before -ly and -ness, which start with a consonant: protectively, protectiveness.

The beat stays on tect

Say them: pro-TECT, pro-TEC-tion, pro-TEC-tive, pro-TEC-tor, un-pro-TEC-ted, o-ver-pro-TEC-tive. The main stress doesn’t move. Endings like -ion and -ive put the stress on the syllable just before them, and in this family that syllable is already tect.

Compare experiment and experimental on the *per- page, where the beat moves and the vowels change. Here the vowels hold, so the family is a good first one for affixes. Learners can add part after part and hear the base stay put.

The o of pro- is weak in all of them, close to the a of about. To hear it, say a word where pro- has the stress: prologue, program.

For the speller

-tion, not -shun. The t belongs to tect: tect + ion. The same is true of act, action and collect, collection.

-or, not -er. Protector and detector take Latin’s -or, as actor, collector and conductor do. It sounds like -er; the spelling marks a Latin word.

One word. Overprotective and unprotected are each one word: a prefix joins the base with no hyphen and no change to either part.
