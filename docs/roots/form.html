Parts · a base family: a word study

formform means ‘a shape’

A shape. Latin forma was a shape, a figure, a mould; English took it through French around 1200. Latin also had infōrmāre, to give shape to, and used it for shaping a mind: to train, to tell. So inform and information are form words, as uniform and transform are.

in + form means put a shape in. in + form + al means not formal.

information is in + form + ation. There is no informate inside it.

Say them: FORM, FOR-mal, for-MA-tion. The beat moves; the part stays form.

Ukrainian has the same word: інформація.

-ation: one part, or three?

Education is educate + -ion: English has the verb educate, so the sum shows it, with the e of -ate dropped. Information has no informate inside it. English took the verb inform through French, not the Latin past form that gave us educate, so the -ate verb never arrived.

So the site writes -ation whole where English has no -ate verb: in + form + ation, form + ation, trans + form + ation. English treats it as one suffix too: it builds flirtation and starvation on verbs that have no -ate form at all (affixes.org).

Inside -ation is Latin’s a + t + io: the vowel of verbs like infōrmāre and ēducāre, the t of the past form, and the noun ending. And informate exists now: Shoshana Zuboff coined it in 1988, from information on the model of automate, for turning work into information. A form the system allows can be made as soon as someone needs it.

Two prefixes spelled in-

Inform and informal start with the same letters and different prefixes. In inform, in- means in, into: to put a shape into a mind. In informal, in- means not: not formal. Latin had both, and English spells both in-. The word’s meaning tells you which one you have.

A cognate: information in Ukrainian

Ukrainian has the same word: інформація (informatsiia). Both come from Latin infōrmātiō. Laid side by side, the parts line up in the same order: in and ін, form and форм, ation and аці.

Two things differ. The t of Latin -tio is sh in English (ti spells /sh/) and ts in Ukrainian (ц spells /ts/). And Ukrainian ends with я, an ending that changes with the word’s job in a sentence; English has none. Both languages stress the same syllable: in-for-MA-tion, ін-фор-МА-ці-я.

Strictly, the two are parallel borrowings of one Latin word rather than cognates inherited side by side. The site uses cognate in the plain sense: words in two languages with one origin.

Look-alikes that aren’t form

Perform looks like per + form and isn’t. It is Old French parfornir, to carry out (par + fornir, the source of furnish), reshaped in Anglo-French under the influence of forme (Etymonline).

The adjective former, earlier, is Old English forma, first, with -er (Dictionary.com). The noun former, one who forms, is form + er and belongs to the family. The two share a spelling and are different words.
