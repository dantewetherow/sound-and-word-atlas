Parts · a base family

signsign means ‘a mark’

A mark, a token. Latin signum was a mark, a sign, a seal, and the standard soldiers followed into battle. English borrowed it again and again. Its spelling keeps a letter that sign doesn’t sound: the g, heard in signal and signature. It’s the classic example of English spelling keeping a part the same when its sound changes.

Word study: The g in sign a presentation for learners, built a piece at a time

These words come from sign. It means a mark.

  • sign
  • signal
  • signature
  • design
  • assign
  • seal

Say them: sign, sig-nal.

In signal you hear the g. In sign you don’t, but it is still there.

It stays so the words look like one family: sign, signal, signature, design.

When the g is silent, the i spells its name: sign, like find.

A seal was a little sign pressed into wax. It lost its g long ago.

How the words came into English

The root came into English four ways, each with its own spellings. This is the time-lapse, or diachronic, view. Pick a spelling to see its words.

sign, g silent: the g kept but not said

sign at the end of a word, or with an English ending after it.

The g sits in its cone: not said, but kept, because signal and signature say it. With the g silent, the i spells its name, as it does in find and child. English endings don’t wake the g: signs, signing, designer, assignment.

sign, g said: the g said

sign with a Latin ending after it: the g closes the syllable, sig, and the i is short.

Here the g is heard: sig-nal, sig-na-ture, sig-ni-fy. These are the words that keep the g in sign. Take it out of sign and the family no longer looks like one.

seal, sig: a little sign

Latin sigillum, a little sign, a seal.

Seal lost the g in French, where sigillum became seel; English took the word without it. Sigil came straight from Latin and kept the g, soft before the i.

→ seala little sign pressed into wax; to close tight
sigila seal or a sign said to have power, in old books and stories

Why sign keeps its g

Say sign and you don’t hear a g. Say signal and you do. The spelling keeps the g in both, so the family looks like a family: sign, signal, signature, design, designate. Carney calls a letter like this inert: silent in one word, said in another (1994: 41). The notation draws it in a cone of silence, joined to neither the i nor the n. So it sits in the same place in every word of the family.

The g also changes the i. When the g is silent, the i spells its name, as in find and child. When the g is said, it closes the syllable, sig, and the i is short. Venezky calls this kind of trade, a long vowel where a consonant is silent, a compensatory alternation.

Chomsky and Halle used sign and signify, malign and malignant, and paradigm and paradigmatic. Their point: English spelling writes the part, not the sound. Melvyn Ramsden and Pete Bowers made sign the first word a class takes apart in structured word inquiry. This page follows that tradition.

Follow or cut?

Under Latin signum sits a root that scholars still argue about. Pokorny, and the Indo-European Lexicon after him, put it with *sekw-, to follow: a sign as the standard soldiers followed. Michiel de Vaan’s Latin dictionary (2008) puts it with *sek-, to cut: a sign as a mark cut into something. A study in the Journal of Latin Linguistics (2025) argues for follow again, on the sounds of signum’s cousins in Oscan.

The family on this page doesn’t depend on the answer: every word in it is built on sign, a mark. That’s why it is a base family here and not a root family. If follow is right, sign is a cousin of sequence, second and pursue.

Signifier and signified

Ferdinand de Saussure built modern linguistics on this word. A linguistic sign, he said, has two sides, joined like the two sides of a sheet of paper. One is the signifier, the sound pattern; the other is the signified, the idea. His French words were signifiant and signifié, the present and past forms of signifier, to signify.

The notation marks the signifier: the sounds and the letters that spell them. The parts and the seams are where the signified shows through: design is de + sign, to mark out, whatever the g is doing.
