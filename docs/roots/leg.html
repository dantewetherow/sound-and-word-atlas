Parts · a root family

*leg-*leg- means ‘gather’

To gather, to collect. Speakers of Greek and Latin carried this Proto-Indo-European root into words about words: gathering words is choosing them, reading them and saying them. English borrowed the family back again and again, and the notation’s own name is one of them.

Sounds first: a circle for each sound.Red for the vowel sounds.A swoop for each syllable: leg, o, mor, phic.Bars where letters spell a single sound: or and ph.Then the letters.A cup over a short vowel, a bar over a long one.Seams where the parts join: leg + o + morph + ic. The o is a part of its own, meaning “I,” so it spells its name, as in go.

These words come from one old word that meant gather.

leg: gather, choose, read

Latin legere, to gather, to choose, to read, at the start of a word.

Elegant kept the e that eligible turned to i.

+ + → legendarytold of in legends
+ → legibleclear enough to read

+ + → elegantchosen with care, graceful

lig: choose, gather

Latin -ligere: after a prefix, legere usually became -ligere (colligere, eligere, diligere), while its past form stayed -lectus.

Lig and lect take turns in the same families: eligible and elect, negligent and neglect, intelligent and intellect. Lig comes before -ent, -ence and -ible, from Latin’s present form; lect comes before -ion, -ive and -or, from its past form.

+ + → eligibleable to be chosen

+ + → negligibletoo small to matter
+ + → intelligentquick to understand
+ + → intelligencethe power to understand
+ + → intelligibleable to be understood
+ + → diligentcareful and hard-working
+ + → diligencecareful, steady work

les, lite: worn down in French

Latin words that reached English through French and lost letters on the way: lectio, a reading, became leçon; and eligere, to pick out, gave eslite, chosen.

lego: I gather

Greek légō and Latin legō, the verb itself, which both languages inherited from the root: in Greek, I gather, I count, I say; in Latin, I gather, I choose, I read.

The ō is the ending for I in both languages, so lego is leg + o, gather + I, as Latin veto is I forbid and credo I believe.

+ + + → legomorphicthis notation’s name: legō, I gather, in Greek and Latin alike, and morph, form
+ + → prolegomenonwhat is said beforehand: a formal introduction to a study. Greek prolegein, to say before; more than one are prolegomena. Prologue is its plainer twin: pro + logue

Marginal or debated

Some words are often listed with the root, but the sources hedge the road back to it. They keep their word sums here, since the parts hold whatever the history. What’s in doubt is only where the root came from.

leg, legis, loy, lege: law

Latin lēx, law; lēgis, of law, as in lēgis lātor, a proposer of law.

Watkins gives this branch as possible only, with a question mark on law as a ‘collection of rules’. He also names a link to another root, *legh-, to lie, to lay, as possible but uncertain: law as what is laid down, which is where English law comes from. Etymonline gives lēx only as perhaps from *leg-.

legis + lat + ure → legislaturethe body that makes laws

leg, lege, league: send with a charge

Latin lēgāre, to send with a charge, to appoint; collēga, one chosen along with another.

Lēgāre is built on lēx, law, so these words share its doubt.

leg: a bean or a pea

Latin legūmen, a pulse: beans, peas and lentils.

Latin legūmen is of unknown origin. That it was named for being picked by hand is one suggestion, nothing firmer.

The root’s spellings

leg
gather, read; send; or law: Latin legere, lēgāre or lēx; in legomorphic, Greek and Latin legō, I gather. In legend, legendary, legible, illegible, legion, elegant, legomorphic, prolegomenon, legal, illegal, delegate, delegation, relegate, legacy, legume.
lect
gathered, chosen, read: Latin lectus, the past form of legere. In collect, collection, collective, collector, recollect, select, selection, selective, elect, election, elector, neglect, neglectful, intellect, lecture, lecturer, dialect, eclectic.
lig
choose, gather: Latin -ligere, legere after a prefix. In eligible, negligent, negligible, intelligent, intelligence, intelligible, diligent, diligence.
log
word, reason, account: Greek logos. In logic, logical, illogical, analogy, apology, apologize, anthology, biology, geology, ecology, psychology, technology.
logue
word, talk: Greek logos. In dialogue, monologue, prologue, epilogue, catalogue, analogue.
lex
word, speech: Greek lexis. In lexicon, lexical, dyslexia.
legis
of law: Latin lēgis. In legislator, legislation, legislature.
loy
law: Latin lēgālis, legal, worn down in French. In loyal, loyalty, disloyal.
lege
chosen with (college), or law (privilege). In privilege, college.
league
chosen with: Latin collēga. In colleague.

Before the root

il-
not, as in illegal; or in, into, as in illuminate: two Latin prefixes spelled in-, both written il- before l. In illegible, illogical, illegal.
e-
out: Latin ex-, written e- before l, d and some other consonants. In elegant, elect, election, elector, eligible.
col-
with, together: Latin com-, written col- before l. In collect, collection, collective, collector, recollect, college, colleague.
re-
again, back. In recollect, relegate.
se-
apart, aside. In select, selection, selective.
neg-
not: Latin neg-, nec. In neglect, neglectful, negligent, negligible.
intel-
between: Latin inter-, written intel- before l. In intellect, intelligent, intelligence, intelligible.
dia-
through, across, between (Greek). In dialect, dialogue.
ec-
out (Greek ek). In eclectic.
di-
apart: Latin dis-, written di- before l. In diligent, diligence.
ana-
up, back, according to (Greek). In analogy, analogue.
apo-
away, off (Greek). In apology, apologize.
mono-
one, alone (Greek). In monologue.
pro-
before, forward, forth: Greek and Latin pro. In prologue, prolegomenon.
epi-
upon, after (Greek). In epilogue.
cata-
down, completely (Greek). In catalogue.
dys-
bad, hard (Greek). In dyslexia.

After the root

-end
to be: Latin legendus, to be read. In legend, legendary.
-ary
having to do with. In legendary.
-ible
able to be. In legible, illegible, eligible, negligible, intelligible.
-ion
the act or result of. In legion, collection, selection, election, legislation, delegation.
-ant
being, doing: from Latin’s present form, as -ent. In elegant.
-ive
tending to, doing. In collective, selective.
-or
one who, the thing that. In collector, elector, legislator.
-ful
full of, given to. In neglectful.
-ure
the act or result of. In lecture, lecturer, legislature.
-er
one who, or the thing that. In lecturer.
-ic
having to do with. In eclectic, logic, logical, illogical, lexicon, lexical, legomorphic.
-ent
being, doing: from Latin’s present form of the verb. In negligent, intelligent, diligent.
-ence
the state of being. In intelligence, diligence.
-al
having to do with. In logical, illogical, lexical, legal, illegal, loyal, loyalty, disloyal.
-y
the Greek ending -ia: a study, an art, a quality; not the y of happy. In analogy, apology, anthology, biology, geology, ecology, psychology, technology.
-ize
make, do. In apologize.
-on
a Greek ending: lexikon, a word book. In lexicon.
-ia
a condition (Greek). In dyslexia.
-o
I: the ending of the verb’s I form in Greek and Latin alike (légō, legō), as in Latin veto, I forbid. In legomorphic.
-omenon
a Greek ending: a thing being done. A phenomenon is a thing appearing; a prolegomenon, a thing being said first. In prolegomenon.
-ty
the state of being. In loyalty.
-ate
a Latin past form: sent, made. In delegate, delegation, relegate.
-acy
a state, an office. In legacy.
-ume
a Latin noun ending (legūmen): a thing. In legume.
