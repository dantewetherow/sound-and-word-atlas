Dolch sight words

All 220 of E. W. Dolch’s sight words (1936), one set for each of his levels, 10 to a page. Each card’s tag names its level and the ELP list that teaches the word, if one does, so you can set aside the cards a class has already met. Where the ELP words come from shows how the two lists line up.

Grade 3 word sums

An extension for students who have finished the ELP lists: 118 cards built on the list words. The front has the whole word. The back has its word sum, such as make + ing → making, and the word coded with a seam at each join. Each group starts on a new sheet, so you can print just the rule you're teaching.

Download the PDF (480 KB, 15 sheets, 10 to a page)

  • Sheets 1–2: words on the lists that are already built from parts (12 cards)
  • Sheets 3–7: endings that leave the base alone (43 cards)
  • Sheet 8: the last consonant doubles (7 cards)
  • Sheets 9–10: the e drops before a vowel, and stays before a consonant (13 cards)
  • Sheet 11: y turns to i, and when it stays (10 cards)
  • Sheets 12–13: prefixes (15 cards)
  • Sheet 14: pairs that aren't word sums (9 cards)

Printing

Print in colour and double-sided, flipping on the long edge, so each back lands behind its front. Print one sheet first and hold it up to a window to check that the backs line up, then cut on the grey lines. Spaces left over on a sheet are blank cards to write on.

Where the words come from

The Early Literacy Profile (ELP) Assessment & Instructional Guide, Central Okanagan Public Schools (2025), pp. 16–17. The guide doesn't name a source for its lists; it says they were aligned with a phonics scope and sequence, keeping one word for each pattern. All but three of its 148 words are E. W. Dolch’s (1936), as Word lists shows. These cards aren't an SD23 publication. The district's own ELP page is the place for the assessment itself.

Word sums

Word sums and word matrices come from Melvyn Ramsden's Real Spelling, and Peter Bowers brought them into classrooms as structured word inquiry. The base words come from the ELP lists and E. W. Dolch's grade 3 list (Dolch, 1936).
