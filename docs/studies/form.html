Find the part they all have.

What could it mean in all of them?

Let the class find form, then guess a meaning that fits every word. A shape works for all of them, information included: a shape put into a mind.

Build it

ininto+forma shape

From the end: a shape put into a mind. To inform is to tell.

Word sum: in + form → inform. Latin infōrmāre meant to give shape to, and was used for shaping a mind: to train, to tell. English took it through Old French enformer (Dictionary.com).

And the noun

ininto+forma shape+ationthe act of

From the end: the act of informing. Information is what informs you.

Word sum: in + form + ation → information. The next slide asks why -ation is one part here.

Where is informate?

an -ate verb inside

no -ate verb inside

educate → education

inform → information

create → creation

form → formation

educate is a word: educat(e) + ion.

informate isn’t: in + form + ation.

No -ate verb? Write -ation as one part.

Latin had ēducāre and infōrmāre, verbs of the same class. English borrowed the past form ēducātus as the verb educate, and took inform through French, so no informate came with it. English also uses -ation on its own verbs: flirtation, starvation (affixes.org). And informate exists now: Shoshana Zuboff coined it in 1988 for turning work into information. Ask the class what it could mean before you tell them.

When the beat leaves form, its or goes weak.

The sound changes. The spelling stays form.

-ation, -al with -ity, and most Latin endings move the main stress onto the syllable just before them. In formation the or of form is weak, close to the er of her. The spelling keeps form, so the word still shows its family.

Two prefixes spelled in-

in-: in, into

in-: not

inform: put a shape in. informal: not formal.

The meaning tells you which in- you have.

Latin had two prefixes spelled in-: one meaning in or into (inform, induce), one meaning not (informal, invisible). English spells both in-. Ask learners to sort a few: include, incorrect, insert, inactive.

A part at the front

trans-: across. Change the form completely.
uni-: one. One form for everyone.
re-: again. Shape it again, better.

Each prefix joins form with no change to either part.

Word sums: trans + form, uni + form, re + form. Platform is plat + form: a flat form. Add the endings next: transformation, uniformity, reformer.

English ti spells /sh/. Ukrainian ц spells /ts/.

Cognates: words in two languages with one origin.

Both words come from Latin infōrmātiō. Each column comes in on a click: in and ін, form and форм, ation and аці, then я. The я at the end changes with the word’s job in a sentence; that’s all learners need about it. Both stress the same syllable: in-for-MA-tion. Strictly the two are borrowings of one Latin word, which the site counts as cognates in the plain sense. Ask what is the same: the parts, in the same order. Learners who speak Ukrainian can say it for the class.
