The g in sign

sign, signal, signature, design, and the family

A word study for learners. Click, tap, or press the right arrow for each next piece; N shows and hides these notes. The page for teachers is sign, with its spelling list.

Say it: sign

There’s a g in it. Can you hear it?

No. So why is it there?

Let learners say sign slowly and listen for a g. The dotted ring is the cone of silence: a letter that is there but not said. Hold the question open; the next slides answer it.

Listen for the g

a light or a sound that tells you something
your name, written by you

In these words, you hear the g.

The same part, sign, in every word, and here the g is said. These are the words that keep the g in sign.

Build signal

signa mark+alto do with

From the end: something to do with a sign. A signal is a sign that sends a message.

Word sum: sign + al → signal. Late Latin signāle, through French. Say it in syllables: sig-nal. The g closes the first syllable, so it is said.

Build signature

signa mark+atedo, make+urewhat comes of it

From the end: what comes of making a mark. Your signature is the mark your name makes.

Word sum: sign + at(e) + ure → signature, from Medieval Latin signātūra. The e of -ate drops before the vowel of -ure, and the t and ure join as they do in picture and nature.

One part, one spelling

Keep the g, and you can see the family.

This is the answer to the first question. Written sine, the word would lose its family. Carney calls a letter like this inert: silent in one word, said in another (A Survey of English Spelling, 1994: 41). The cone keeps the g in the same place in every word of the family.

When the g is quiet, the i says its name

sig·nal

When the g is said, it closes the syllable: sig. The i is short.

Sign, find, child: the i says its name with no e after it to make it long (Carney’s rules I.11 and I.12). In signal the g closes the syllable and the i is short. Venezky calls this a compensatory alternation: the vowel is long where the consonant goes quiet.

Build design

deout+signmark

From the end: mark it out. A design is a plan, marked out.

Word sum: de + sign → design, from Latin dēsignāre, to mark out. The g is quiet again: sign ends the word.

The g stays quiet

someone who designs
to give someone a job
a job you are given
to give up a job

The endings -er and -ment don’t wake the g up.

English endings after sign leave the g silent: signs, signing, designer, assignment. Assign is as + sign: Latin ad-, to, written as- before the s, so each part brings its own s. Resign is re + sign: to sign back, to give up.

Build signify

signa mark+ijoins+fymake

From the end: make a sign. To signify is to show, to mean.

Word sum: sign + i + fy → signify, from Latin significāre, to make a sign. The i is a connecting vowel. Saussure’s signifier and signified come from this verb: the sound of a word, and the idea it stands for.

The g is said again

full of meaning: it matters
a name or a title

someone who signs an agreement

A Latin ending after sign, and you hear the g.

sign + i + fic + ant; de + sign + at(e) + ion; re + sign + at(e) + ion; sign + at(e) + ory. In designation and resignation the s says z and the e is short: des-ig-na-tion.

A little sign that lost its g

sigillumLatin: a little sign

seelFrench: the g worn away

A seal was a little sign, pressed into wax.

Latin sigillum, a little sign, wore down in Old French to seel, and English took it without the g. Sigil, a seal or a sign of power, came straight from Latin and kept it. The sea animal is a different word altogether, from Old English seolh.

For teachers

Word sums: sign + al → signal; sign + at(e) + ure → signature; de + sign → design; de + sign + er → designer; as + sign + ment → assignment; sign + i + fy → signify; sign + i + fic + ant → significant.

The g follows Carney and Venezky, as the site ruled on 12 November 2026; Chomsky and Halle used sign and signify for the same point. The root under Latin signum is debated, follow or cut.
