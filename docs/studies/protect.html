Give the class time before anything is explained. Most will find protect quickly. Ask what the rest of each word adds, and keep their guesses: the slides that follow test them.

Build it

proin front+tectcovered

Read it from the end: covered in front. To protect is to keep safe.

Word sum: pro + tect → protect. Latin prōtegere, to cover in front, to shield: prō, in front, and tegere, to cover.

An old word for cover

*(s)teg-to cover, often with a roof
Latin tegereto cover
Latin tectuscovered: the part in protect

tect means covered.

The root is *(s)teg-, to cover (Indo-European Lexicon, after Pokorny; Etymonline). English has words from it by other roads: thatch and deck (Germanic), tile and toga (Latin), stegosaurus (Greek stegos, a roof). They are on the *(s)teg- page. This study stays with tect.

Why tect, and not teg?

Latin’s word for cover was tegere, with a g.

Say /g/, then /k/. Your tongue is in the same place.

Before a t, Latin’s g turned into c.

Latin tegere, tectus; legere, lectus; regere, rectus. Allen and Greenough give the rule: g before t became c (§15.10). /k/ is /g/ without the voice, and the t that followed has no voice either. English borrowed the past forms, so the parts are tect, lect and rect.

Add an ending. Watch the word.

protect+ s: does it now+ ed: already done+ ing: doing it now

Count the beats: pro-tect, pro-tects, pro-tec-ted, pro-tec-ting.

Word sums: pro + tect + s, pro + tect + ed, pro + tect + ing. Each word fades into the next at the same size, so only the new ending and its marks change. Ask which one adds a beat that -s doesn’t.

After a t or a d, -ed adds a beat.

The spelling stays -ed every time.

-ed spells /t/ after a sound made without the voice (p, k, s, f, sh, ch) and /d/ after one made with it, vowels included. After t or d it adds a syllable, so protected has three. Spelling these jumpt or playd would lose the ending, and with it the meaning: already done.

Name the act

proin front+tectcovered+ionthe act of

From the end: the act of covering in front. Protection keeps you safe.

Word sum: pro + tect + ion → protection. A suffix that starts with a vowel joins tect with no change to its spelling.

Spell the part, not the sound: tect + ion.

Watch the t: a black circle in protect, and a green bar under ti in protection. The green bar marks one sound spelled with two letters. The t never leaves the spelling, so protection still shows protect. A learner who writes protecshun has spelled the sound and lost the word.

Two more endings

+ ive: that protects
+ or: one who protects

-or sounds like -er. It marks a Latin ending: actor, collector.

Word sums: pro + tect + ive → protective; pro + tect + or → protector. Latin’s agent ending is -or, and words built on Latin parts usually keep it: actor, collector, conductor, detector. -er is English’s own: teacher, painter.

Why is there an e on -ive?

The e is silent. The i stays short.

English words don’t end in v: have, give, live, active.

The e is there because the word would end in v.

The marks come in a layer at a time: the circles, the cup on the short i, then the e that isn’t said. The e of -ive makes no vault: the i is short. English spelling avoids a final v, so give, have and live carry an e that does nothing to the vowel. A few short new words break it, such as rev.

Keep going

protective+ ly: in a protective way+ ness: being protective

-ly and -ness start with a consonant. The e stays.

Before a vowel, the e drops: active, activity.

Word sums: pro + tect + ive + ly → protectively; pro + tect + ive + ness → protectiveness. The usual rule for a final e: it drops before a suffix that starts with a vowel, as in act + iv(e) + ity → activity. It stays before one that starts with a consonant.

A part at the front

unnot+protectkeep safe+eddone

un- means not: not protected.

Word sum: un + pro + tect + ed → unprotected. A prefix joins with no change to either part. The -ed still adds its beat after the t: un-pro-tec-ted.

And another

overtoo much+protectkeep safe+ivethat tends to

over- means too much: protective, too much.

Word sum: over + pro + tect + ive → overprotective. One word, no hyphen. Two prefixes now stand before tect, and each keeps its spelling.

The beat stays on tect every time.

The o of pro is weak, like the a in about.

-ion and -ive put the main stress on the syllable just before them, and in this family that syllable is tect already. So nothing moves, and the vowels hold. Compare experiment and experimental in Try it out, where the beat moves. The o of pro- is clear where pro- has the stress: prologue, program.

Take the cover off

deoff, away+tectcovered

From the end: the cover taken off. To detect is to find out.

Word sum: de + tect → detect. Latin dētegere, to uncover. A detective was first a detective police officer: one whose job was finding out.

The same endings on detect

detect+ ive: one whose job is finding out+ ion: the finding out+ or: a thing that finds

The same sound changes come with each ending.

Word sums: de + tect + ive, de + tect + ion, de + tect + or; and un + de + tect + ed → undetected. Ask learners to say which sound changes before you click: the ti of detection, the silent e of detective.
