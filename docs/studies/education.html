A word study · *deuk-

From duct to education

a word that leads, and the words built on it

A word study for learners. Click, tap, or press the right arrow for each next piece; N shows and hides these notes. The page for teachers is *deuk-, with its spelling list.

A duct leads something along

A duct leads air through a building. An aqueduct leads water.

duct, and its partner duce: lead.

Latin dūcere, to lead, and ductus, led. English borrowed the verbs with duce and their nouns with duct. The root is *deuk-, to lead, to pull: English kept its own words from it too, tug and tow.

duce and duct take turns

producelead forward: make
productwhat is made
reducelead back: make less
reductionthe making less

The verb says duce. Add an ending, and it says duct.

duce is Latin’s present form, duct its past form: produce, product, production; reduce, reduction. The front part says which way the leading goes: pro, forward; re, back; intro, inside; con, together.

ex- means out. Before d, it gets shorter.

exout+duclead

Try saying ex-duc. Now e-duc. The x drops out: it’s easier to say.

Same part, shorter spelling: e- means out.

Latin ex- is written e- before d, l, m, n, r and v: educate, emerge, elect, evict. The same thing happens inside Latin itself: it said ēdūcere, to lead out, not exdūcere.

-ate and -ion

decorateto make it pretty
decorationwhat you make
celebrateto have a party
celebrationthe party

-ate makes a doing word. -ion names the doing.

Both are Latin. -ate is the past form of a verb, made into an English verb; -ion makes a noun of an act. When -ion joins -ate, the e drops and the t says sh: decorate, decoration.

The e drops out. The i takes over its job.

the e at the end makes the a say its name
no e now: the i of -ion does the same job

Letters can drop out, and others do their jobs.

The notation draws it: in notate the vault comes from the final e; in notation the e is gone and the vault comes from the i of -ion, which stands before another vowel (the pattern on the *per- page). The t says sh before i and a vowel: the green bar under ti.

Build it

eout+duclead, bring up+atedo it

Read it from the end: do the bringing up, the leading out. To educate is to bring someone up and on.

Word sum: e + duc + ate → educate. Latin ēducāre, to bring up, to rear, a verb beside ēdūcere, to lead out. To educate was first to raise a child; leading out what is in a learner is a later reading of the word, and a fair one (the *deuk- page).

Name the doing

eout+duclead+atedo it+ionthe doing

From the end: the doing of bringing up and leading out.

Word sum: e + duc + at(e) + ion → education. The e of -ate drops before -ion, and the i of -ion takes over its job, making the a long. The t says sh.

One more part

educationthe bringing up+alhaving to do with

From the end: having to do with the bringing up. An educational game helps you learn.

Word sum: e + duc + at(e) + ion + al → educational. Each ending turns the word into a new kind: a doing word (-ate), a thing (-ion), a describing word (-al). Read from the last part back, they come in order.

And one at the front

mistake, misspell: done wrongly.

miswrongly+educationthe bringing up

mis- means wrongly: miseducation is teaching that leads you wrong.

Word sum: mis + e + duc + at(e) + ion → miseducation. A prefix joins at the front with no change: mis + spell keeps both s’s, misspell.

Say them. The beat moves.

ED·u·cate

ed·u·CA·tion

ed·u·CA·tion·al

The beat moves, and the spelling stays the same. Listen to the d, too: many people say ED-ju-cate, and the spelling keeps the d of duc.

-ion pulls the stress to the syllable just before it: EDucate, eduCAtion, eduCAtional. The a is long in all three. The d and the u blend into the sound of j in most speech (as in gradual and procedure); the spelling keeps the parts, and the notation has no mark for the blend.

English kept its own: pulling together
Team is English’s own: in the Germanic languages the root’s d became t, so tug, tow, tie and team start with t where Latin’s words start with d. The whole family is on the *deuk- page.

For teachers

Word sums: e + duc + ate → educate; e + duc + at(e) + ion → education; e + duc + at(e) + ion + al → educational; mis + e + duc + at(e) + ion → miseducation; pro + duce, pro + duct, re + duct + ion.

The family follows the Indo-European Lexicon (deuk-), checked against Etymonline; educate is from Latin ēducāre, to bring up. The teacher’s page is *deuk-; the list to print is the *deuk- spelling list.
