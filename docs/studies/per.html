Two long words. What’s the same?

experiment

experience

The same start. A different end.

Both are ex + peri, with a different ending: -ment and -ence. The next three slides give each part a felt meaning from words learners know, before the parts go back together.

ex-

exita way out
exhalebreathe out
exportsend out
explodeburst out

ex- means out.

Latin ex, out of; in some words, all the way, thoroughly. Before some letters it shrinks to e-: educate, emerge. The examples are set in type: the site has no checked marks for them yet.

-ment

pay → paymentthe paying
move → movementthe moving
agree → agreementwhat you agree
enjoy → enjoymentwhat you get from it

-ment: the doing of it, or what comes of it.

Latin -mentum: the act, the means or the result. It makes a noun from a verb.

-ence

silent → silencethe state of being silent
patient → patiencethe state of being patient
absent → absencethe state of being away
different → differencethe state of being different

-ent: being that way. -ence: the state of being that way.

-ent is Latin’s present form, -ens, -entis: silens, being silent; absens, being away. -ence, from Latin -entia, makes a noun of that: the state of being silent, a silence. So silent describes, and silence names. Ask learners which word fits: The room was ___ (silent). We sat in ___ (silence).

per: try, take a risk

someone who has tried, and knows
a risk, a danger
someone who tries an attack
what a risk makes you feel

A very old word for try, in many words.

All four come from the root *per-, to try, to risk: expert from Latin expertus, tried; peril from perīculum, a trial, a danger; pirate from Greek peira, a trial; fear from Old English fær, a danger, a word English kept from the start.

Build it

exout+peritry+mentthe doing

Read it from the end: the doing of trying it out. An experiment is a trying-out.

Word sum: ex + peri + ment → experiment. Read from the last part back, the parts give the meaning in order: the suffix says what kind of word it is (a thing, an act), and the rest says what of.

And the other one

exout+peritry+encebeing that way

From the end: being one who has tried things out. Experience is what trying leaves you with.

Word sum: ex + peri + ence → experience. In Latin, experientia was the trying and the knowing that comes of it. English once used the two words almost the same way; French still uses one, expérience, for both.

Listen to the e

ex·per·i·ment

In per, the r closes the syllable. The e is short.

ex·pe·ri·ence

In pe, the syllable is open. The e says its name.

The notation draws this as closed and open: per in experiment, as in very; pe in experience, with the vault from the i, as in here. The patterns underneath (Chomsky and Halle) are on the teacher’s page, with their exceptions.

And the i?

experiment

experience

Both words have an i after per. It’s left over from the old Latin word.

You don’t need to know why it’s there to read them. The family is what matters.

The i is Latin’s theme vowel: experīrī is a verb of the class whose stems end in ī (audīre: audience, auditor). It marks the verb’s class and carries no meaning. Expert, from expertus, has none: the root is per. In experience it also does a job in sound, making the e long. None of this is needed to teach the morphology, phonology, spelling or history here.

For teachers

Word sums: ex + peri + ment → experiment; ex + peri + ence → experience; ex + pert → expert; peril + ous → perilous; em + pir + ic + al → empirical.
