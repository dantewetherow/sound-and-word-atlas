What happens when a vowel suffix joins, and joins again

+ ing →

Step 15 of the route: the e drops, its jobs move

The words: make, making, have, having, race, racing, ice, icy.

Where did the e go, and who is doing its work?

The convention

When a suffix that starts with a vowel joins a base that ends in a silent e, the old vowel letter goes and the new one takes its place.

The e isn’t lost for nothing. It was doing a job from the end of the word, and the suffix’s vowel now stands where it stood.

So the new vowel takes over the job.

The new vowel does the old one’s jobs

make + ing

The e of make sent the vault that keeps the a long. In making the i sends it.

race + ing

The e of race also nudged the c soft. The i does both.

ice + y

A y can do it too: it is a vowel letter here.

The same join, in short words and long

hope + ing→ hoping
note + ate→ notate
create + ion→ creation
produce + er→ producer
free + ed→ freednot freeed
see + en→ seennot seeen

With ee, one e goes: English doesn’t write three in a row.

When the old stays

hope + less → hopeless

The suffix starts with a consonant. Nothing new arrives to take the e’s job, so the e keeps it.

notice + able → noticeable

The new vowel can’t do the job. An a doesn’t nudge c soft, so the e stays to do it. Changeable keeps its e for the g.

see + ing → seeing

The e is half of a team, ee. Drop it and seing would read as ei. In being the e is the base’s own vowel.

What was the e doing,
and can the newcomer do it?

If it can, the e goes. If it can’t, or nothing new arrives, the e stays.

Suffixes stack, and each join is made fresh

e + duc + ate→ educate
e + duc + ate + ion→ educationout once
e + duc + ate + ion + al→ educationalnothing to drop
e + duc + ate + or→ educatorout once

A long word isn’t a long string of letters. It is a short word that has been joined to, more than once, by the same convention each time.

Out twice: note, notate, notation

→→

note + ate

note + ate + ion

Each time, the vault that kept a vowel long is sent by the newcomer: the a of ate for the o, then the i of ion for that a.

Longer stacks

pro + duct + ive + ity→ productivity
ac + com + mod + ate + ion→ accommodation
lect + ure + er→ lecturer
as + simil + ate + ion→ assimilation

The prefixes stack at the front by their own conventions. The suffixes stack at the back by this one.

The move: peel it back

Take the suffixes off from the end, one at a time, and put each e back.

accommodation− ion, + e→ accommodate
productivity− ity, + e→ productive
productive− ive→ product

A reader finds a word they know inside the long one. A speller finds where the letters come from.

A caution: the parts stay, the sounds move

Stacking a suffix often moves the stress, and the vowels change with it: EDucate, eduCAtion. The spelling of each part doesn’t change.

So a word sum explains a spelling better than it predicts a pronunciation. That is a reason to teach it for spelling first.

When a learner writes makeing, ask what the e was doing, and who is doing it now.
