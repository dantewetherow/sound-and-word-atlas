The gymnasium

A gym for each step on the path. Build sentences from the step’s words on a balance, tip them from a claim to a question, and unlock a round at a time as a learner decodes. Tap any word to walk it.

For teachers: how the gym works
  • Read first. Change a word, have the learner read the whole sentence aloud, then press Check it. The verdict stays hidden until they've read it.
  • Word cards. Every word the gym uses is on the word cards, plain on one side and coded on the other, in a set for each step.
  • Unlocking. Each step on the path has a gym, and each gym has rounds. When a learner decodes everything in a round, press Decoded! to unlock the next one: a new slot, or a slot coming loose. You can also jump to any round. After the last round, On to step opens the next step’s gym at its first round; steps stay open, so you choose when a learner is ready for the next one. Each round keeps the words from the one before.
  • Not (step 4). Add not puts it at the start of the line below: it doesn’t tip, so it stays put when the beam does (Is he not in it?). Squeeze it joins it to the operator, so it hops up onto the beam and tips with it (Isn’t he in it?). Am has no n’t, so I am not can’t squeeze; the question borrows aren’t (Aren’t I in it?). Squeezing the actor and operator together (I’m, he’s, we’re) is a different squeeze, for later.
  • Did, can, will (step 6). These sit on the beam and tip, and the action on the line below stays plain: Did she go in it? Went carries its own time, so with went the beam is empty and there’s nothing to tip. Did she went? is a crash: two words both carrying the time.
  • Nouns (steps 4 to 6). Dolch’s nouns come into the gym at the step where they become decodable: dog, cat, pig, bed, leg at step 4, with my; man, men, box, sun at step 5, with the, this and that; bell, grass, hill, milk, nest, stick at step 6. The determiner has its own slot before the noun. Tap either word to walk it.
  • Could, would, should (step 7) join can, will, must on the beam, each with a plain action below, and each squeezes: couldn’t, wouldn’t, shouldn’t.
  • Have, give, live (step 8). Have is the plain form has was waiting for: I have it, Did she have it? Give and gave bring a second slot for who gets it: She gave him the box. Only things are given, never it or a person. Live takes a position and a place: I live in it beside I am in it.
  • Which (step 5). When the question word is the actor, it asks the question itself, so the beam doesn’t tip: Which is in it? Questions where which is the place (Which is she in?) come later.
  • The beam holds the actor and the operator (am, is, are). Tip it to turn a claim into a question. Actions like go and hit sit on the second line and can't tip yet: that needs do, later on the path.
  • Four verdicts. Works: standard English. Almost: the meaning is clear and one word changes it to standard English. Many people say these at home; the board doesn't call them wrong, it shows the standard English way. Missing a piece: nothing is wrong, something is still to come. Crash: the pieces fight each other, and the board says why.
  • Giggle-free. This is a game you play with a learner, not one they sit alone with, so some sentences never come up: nothing goes in or on a person, a body or an animal; people and animals only follow by or with; nobody hits a person or an animal; my only goes with nouns it can’t turn into something else; and my bed is only ever yours. Silly is fine (The pig is by the box). When a change would make one of those sentences, the board moves the other word along.
  • Marks. The board starts in plain text. Turn the marks on one layer at a time, in the order the word walk brings them in (vowel colours, bars, sound circles, vowel marks, swoops, parts), or press All. Every visit starts plain again. Tapping a word still walks it with every mark.
  • Walk a word. Tap any word on the board to decode it a step at a time: the letters, a circle for each sound, the vowel marks, then the sounds put together. It’s the same walk as the Word walk page. Tap the word, swipe it left, or press Next to go on; swipe right to go back.
  • Hard to picture. Some sentences work but are hard to picture (I am in me). Ask what it would look like. They're good for talk and drawing.
