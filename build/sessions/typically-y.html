Where y spells a vowel, and the one place it doesn’t

The saying

The vowels are a, e, i, o, u, and sometimes y.

It is true that y does two jobs. The trouble is the word sometimes, which makes the vowel job sound like the rare one.

How often is sometimes?

A count

Of the 20,000 commonest English words, 2,197 have a y in them.

begin with y75about 3 in 100
end in y1,581about 72 in 100

A y at the start of a word is the consonant. Nearly every other y is a vowel.

In running text the consonant shows up more, about one y-word in five, because you, your, year and yes are so common. The words are few; they are just said a lot.

The one place y is a consonant

At the start of a word or a syllable, before a vowel.

Inside a single word part it is rare: canyon, beyond. In lawyer it starts the ending.

The notation draws this y in black, as a consonant, with a black circle under it.

Everywhere else,
y spells a vowel.

It has four vowel jobs, and where it sits tells you which.

1. Alone at the end of a short word: the name of i

The syllable is open, so the vowel spells its name, as the e of me and the o of go do. The y is red, with a long mark.

In a longer word the same y has the stress: reply, deny, July.

2. At the end of a longer word: the long e sound

It is said as long e, the sound of me.

Often it is a part of its own, the suffix -y or -ly: hap + p + y, fun + n + y, on(e) + ly.

3. In a team, where the team ends a word

inside a wordraincoinvein
at the endrayboythey

In these teams y is the partner of i: i inside the word, y at its end.

4. Inside a word, mostly in words from Greek

short, as i in him

gym, system, symbol, crystal

long, as i in time

style, cycle, hyphen, rhyme

what it shows

Here y is doing exactly what i does, with the same marks. The letter is a sign of where the word came from.

Y and i trade places

Because they do the same vowel work, one can stand in for the other when a suffix joins.

happy + er→ happiery to i
try + ed→ triedy to i
die + ing→ dyingi to y
lie + ing→ lyingi to y

One test, for a reader

Is the y at the start of a syllable, with a vowel after it? Then it is a consonant. Anywhere else, it is a vowel.

And for a speller: at the end of a word, write y for the name of i, and for long e at the end of a longer word.
