# Sound and Word Atlas

A route through English spelling for teachers: 32 steps in eight bands, with every word drawn in Legomorphic Notation. Each step has its own page with its words marked, the marks it brings in, its kernel words and contrasts, and the pages in Edward Carney's *A Survey of English Spelling* (Routledge, 1994) that back it.

Since 8 November 2026 the pages sit behind eight doors, named as one word family: Progressions (the route), Notations (the marks), Explorations (words to take apart and build), Constructions (sentences), Derivations (roots), and three doors for teachers, Instructions (presentations and cards), Connections (crosswalks to the ELP and Dolch lists, UFLI Foundations and Fry's words) and Explanations (definitions, about and credits). Every page has breadcrumbs, the site map lists them all, and a search box finds words, steps, marks, terms and pages. A Teacher/Student switch beside Bright/Night shows the same pages two ways: student view shows the five doors with a short word each (Steps, Marks, Words, Sentences, Roots), draws a step's words big for the board, and hides the teacher's notes and the under-construction notes.

The site is at https://legomorphics.ca/. The old address, https://dantewetherow.github.io/sound-and-word-atlas/, forwards there.

The domains are registered at Porkbun, which also holds their DNS. legomorphics.ca points at GitHub Pages (four A records, four AAAA records, and www as a CNAME to dantewetherow.github.io), and the TXT record that verifies it with GitHub (`_github-pages-challenge-dantewetherow`) has to stay. legomorphics.com forwards permanently to legomorphics.ca, and logomorphics.ca and logomorphics.com forward there for now with temporary redirects. `docs/CNAME` holds the custom domain: the builds leave it alone, and it shouldn't be deleted.

## What's where

- `docs/` is the site itself. GitHub Pages publishes this folder as it is.
- `build/` has what makes it:
  - `build_site.py` writes the pages from `data/route.json`. Every page has a Bright/Night switch: it follows the device until a reader picks, and keeps the pick in that browser.
  - `doors.py` holds the seven doors and the tree (`PAGES`: which page sits behind which door), and writes the door pages, the site map, the breadcrumbs and `search.json`. It also holds the Teacher/Student switch, which keeps its pick in the browser the same way; a link ending `?view=student` opens a page in student view. In the markup, `t-only` marks what student view hides and `s-only` what only student view shows. Search runs in the reader's browser over `search.json`, so nothing typed leaves the device. A word lands on the word itself: its figure on the first step that has it, its place in the Word lists table, or, for Dolch's nouns, its page of the actor cards (`word_id` gives each its id). Several words typed together are also looked up one at a time, and a long group of results has a Show all button. Without script, pages show the teacher view, the doors stay open, and the header's Site map link stands in for search. Old links to the home page's `#route`, `#band-…` and `#marks` are sent on to Progressions and Notations.
  - `walk.py` writes the Word walk (`walk.html`, behind Explorations): type a word and tap through it. *Read it* starts from the graphemes and decodes (sounds, vowel marks, jobs, syllables, parts); *Spell it* starts from the syllables, then the sounds, then the graphemes. Every marked word on the route, the ELP and Dolch lists (nouns too) and the word families is drawn once with its layer classes into `docs/walk/<id>.json`, which the page fetches when it's asked for. Words the site still shows in plain letters aren't in it, and any other word would need an automatic first pass that would get some wrong.
  - `connections.py` writes the Connections door and its UFLI crosswalk (`ufli.html`). UFLI's copyright statement allows original work made to line up with its scope and sequence and asks for links rather than copies, so the crosswalk gives lesson numbers and our own descriptions, never UFLI's lesson names, from `data/ufli_crosswalk.json`. Fry's lists are still under copyright, so the door compares them in counts and links to the publisher, and the site makes no Fry cards.
  - `fonts/` holds the site's fonts, served from the site since 8 November 2026 so a page asks nothing of anyone but its host: Fraunces and Lexend (variable) and Gentium Plus, from Fontsource 5.3.0, each with its SIL Open Font License. `fonts.css` declares them, and the build copies the folder to `docs/fonts/`. The web copies of the decks use them too; the offline copies still build their fonts in.
  - `parse_route.py` turns the manual's route section (`data/route_section.md`) into `data/route.json`.
  - `x2t.py` converts a read of the manual from the Claude Docs connector into markdown.
  - `legomorphic_words.py` draws the marked words. Since 7 November 2026 the swoop sits 0.85 of a letter size under the baseline (it was 0.73) and a word drawing is 2.48 letter sizes tall (it was 2.36): `SWOOP` and `HEIGHT` set both, and the card and handout builders read `HEIGHT`. The deck, the logo and the wordmark were lowered by hand the same day. On 8 November 2026 the vaults were trimmed: a vault sent from an i or j leaves above its dot, and where a vowel takes a vault and sends one (the a of *notate* and *notation*), the arrow lands right of centre and the next vault leaves left of it, so the two don't meet. The same day, vowel letters became red inside units too (the *u* of *buy*, the *i* of *notation*, the *u* of *squirrel*), and `g.ti` draws the sh sound spelled *ti* with a green bar and circle. The same day *qu* got a unit of its own, `q.qu`: a black bond bar under both letters, since *q* never comes without *u*, and a black circle under each, since they say k and w, with the *u* red (*squirrel*, on the actor cards).
  - `known_codes.py` and `deck_codes.json` hold the codes the session decks already use, and `site_codes.py` holds the codes written for route words that had none.
  - `build_elp_cards.py`, `build_dolch_cards.py` and `build_word_sum_cards.py` make the printable cards. `dolch_codes.py` holds the codes written for the 43 Dolch words that had none.
  - `word_lists.py` writes the Word lists page, which shows where the ELP words come from in Dolch's lists. Its words come from `data/elp_hfw.json` and `data/dolch.json`, and every count on it is worked out from them.
  - `sessions.py` writes the Presentations page and copies the decks in `sessions/` to the site. The decks there are the web copies: the title slide's byline is left off, and a link in the slide controls leads back to the page. Run as a script, `python3 build/sessions.py <node_modules> docs` makes each deck's offline copy (with `make_offline_deck.py`, which needs `@fontsource-variable/fraunces`, `@fontsource-variable/lexend` and `@fontsource/gentium-plus`) and its preview pictures.
  - `build_handouts.py` makes the Session 1 handouts: `python3 build/build_handouts.py <node_modules> docs/sessions/handouts <a scratch folder>`. It is the atlas tool with one change, a footer that names the session instead of the presenter. It also makes the modal cards, the operator cards' first version (8 October), with that day's marking (a doubled letter boxed, not barred) and their instruction sheet as it was; they were rebuilt on 8 November 2026 so the swoop sits where it does everywhere else.
  - `build_role_cards.py` makes the action cards (Dolch's verbs that aren't verb-system cards) and the actor cards (twelve pronouns and Dolch's 95 nouns) the same way: `python3 build/build_role_cards.py <node_modules> docs/sessions/handouts <a scratch folder>`. It holds the codes written for the nouns and the calls they rest on, which wait under "From the action and actor cards" in the Marking calls tab.
  - `build_verb_cards.py` makes the verb-system cards from `build_handouts.py`'s pieces, with the codes the notation didn't have and the calls they rest on: `python3 build/build_verb_cards.py <node_modules> docs/sessions/handouts <a scratch folder>`.
  - `landing.py` writes the landing section at the top of the home page: *legomorphic* built up mark by mark, the way the decks build a word, and *note* to *notate* to *notation*, with the e dropping at each seam. Without script, or with reduced motion, it shows the finished words. The codes for its words and the calls they rest on are in `site_codes.py`.
  - `logo.py` draws the wordmark, λέγω · morphic, from the fonts as paths, so the file stands on its own, and writes it to `build/wordmark.svg`, which the About page draws in with the site's colours. It writes the logo, *go* with its letters lifted out, to `build/logo.svg`, which the site copies as the tab icon, `favicon.svg`, with PNG icons (`favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`). It also draws the other wordmark sketch (λέγ ō morph ic) and the logo candidates (go, it, leg, note) into an out dir: `python3 build/logo.py <node_modules> <out dir>`. The d b q p logo came off the site on 6 November 2026.
  - `characters.py` draws the characters: a word with its letters lifted out, the gap they leave closed, cropped to its marks. Note, the mascot, is *note*, and sits beside the site's name in the header; the Dialogue Team is She, Could and Go, who make a claim and then flip it into a question. A face's mouth, the swoop, sits `MOUTH` letter sizes lower than a word's swoop (since 8 November 2026), and the tab icon in `logo.py` uses the same drop.
  - `hop_hope_codes.py` holds the draft codes for *hop* and *hope*, the first concept to be built end to end, with the calls they rest on. The site doesn't use them until they're ruled.
  - `definitions.py` writes the Definitions page: the entry that coins *legomorphic*, with *legomorph* and *legomorphics*, then the words for sentences, marks and teaching, each marked coined or adopted, with its source. The marks come from the legend, and Note, the mascot, is *note* with its letters lifted out.
  - `about_pages.py` writes the About page, with the wordmark beside the etymology, and the Credits page, with the copyright notice and the contact link.
  - `site.css` is the stylesheet.

## The manual stays the source

The route and its notes come from the *Legomorphic Notation* manual. To change what the site says, change the manual, then rebuild. Choices about how a word is marked wait for a ruling in the manual's Marking calls tab, under "From the atlas site", and `site_codes.py` changes to match once they're ruled.

The site keeps notes about the build off its pages. Anything still being worked out shows in one form, *(Under construction: what's unsettled. Next: what comes next.)*, made by `uc()` in `build_site.py`. The manual's open items show that way on the steps they name, from `UC_OPEN`; a new or changed open item stops the build until it has its note there. The words shown in plain letters take theirs from `UNMARKED` in `site_codes.py`.

The cards and the site carry no colours for the sentence roles (since 8 November 2026): the card that moves in a flip is outlined in ink. The Session 1 deck still colours actors, actions and operators.

## Rebuilding

1. Read the manual's route section (from "The route" to "The operator system") with the Claude Docs connector, save the result, and convert it with `build/x2t.py`. Keep the part before "## The operator system" as `build/data/route_section.md`.
2. `python3 build/parse_route.py build/data/route_section.md build/data/route.json`
3. To rebuild the word cards, run `npm install @fontsource-variable/lexend` somewhere outside the repository, then `python3 build/build_elp_cards.py <that folder>/node_modules docs/cards <a scratch folder>`. The words come from `build/data/elp_hfw.json`, taken from the SD23 ELP guide (pp. 16–17). The grade 3 word sum cards build the same way with `build/build_word_sum_cards.py`; their word sums come from the manual's Word analysis draft tab and their markings from `build/word_sum_codes.py`, whose `CALLS` wait for a ruling in the manual's Marking calls tab. The Dolch cards build the same way with `build/build_dolch_cards.py`, in six sets: his five levels and, since 8 November 2026, his 95 nouns, whose codes are in `build/noun_codes.py` (shared with the actor cards). Their words come from `build/data/dolch.json`, which holds E. W. Dolch's 220 words (1936) at the levels ReadWriteThink prints, with his *going* where its list has *giving*, and his 95 nouns. The codes the decks don't have come from `build/dolch_codes.py`, whose `CALLS` wait under "From the Dolch cards" in the Marking calls tab.
4. `python3 build/build_site.py build/data/route.json docs --standalone`. It writes the Word cards page when the card PDFs are in `docs/cards`, and the Word lists page.
5. Commit and push. Pages republishes the site within a few minutes.

To refresh the deck codes from a copy of the atlas folder, run `python3 build/known_codes.py --refresh /path/to/atlas`.

Without `--standalone`, the home page is written with no doctype or head, which is what the claude.ai artifact version of the site needs.
